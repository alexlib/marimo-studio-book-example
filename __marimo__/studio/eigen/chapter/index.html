Chapter 1

The Shape of a Matrix

A symmetric matrix stretches the plane. Its eigenvectors are the two directions it cannot rotate, and its eigenvalues say how hard it pulls along each one.

Every symmetric matrix factors into three simpler pieces: a rotation, a stretch, and the rotation undone.

Here V holds the eigenvectors as columns and D is diagonal, holding the eigenvalues. This chapter makes the formula physical: you choose the matrix, and the page shows the stretch it performs.

What it does to a circle

Apply M to every point of the unit circle and you get an ellipse. The ellipse's axes point along the eigenvectors, and each axis is as long as the matching eigenvalue. That is the entire geometric content of the eigendecomposition.
