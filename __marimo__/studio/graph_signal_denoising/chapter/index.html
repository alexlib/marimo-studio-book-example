Chapter 3

Filtering on Graphs

The Laplacian's eigenvectors are a Fourier basis for a graph. Signals live at the smooth end; noise spreads everywhere. Keep the smooth end, and the noise falls away.

A graph signal assigns a number to every node of a graph — a temperature to every sensor, a measurement to every point. Chapter 2 showed that low Laplacian eigenvectors vary smoothly across edges while high ones oscillate. This chapter uses that split as a filter: expand a noisy measurement in the eigenbasis, discard the rough components, and reconstruct.

A signal on a circle

The notebook samples points on a circle, joins each to its nearest neighbors, and measures a signal at every node — then corrupts it with Gaussian noise. You control all four ingredients.

Filter in the eigenbasis

The eigenvectors v1, …, vn of the Laplacian form an orthonormal basis, so any signal f is a unique combination of them. Denoising is a projection: keep only the first k terms.
